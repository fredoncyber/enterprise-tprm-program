# TPRM Program Charter

## 1. Purpose
The Third-Party Risk Management program provides enterprise governance and repeatable processes to identify, assess, treat, monitor, and report risks arising from vendors, suppliers, contractors, affiliates, cloud providers, software producers, data processors, managed service providers, and material fourth parties.

## 2. Scope
The program applies to prospective, active, renewing, materially changed, and terminating third parties that can affect the confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy, safety, compliance, finances, operations, reputation, or strategic objectives of the organization.

## 3. Authority
The Board or delegated risk committee approves risk appetite. Executive management provides resources. The TPRM function may require evidence, impose conditions, escalate overdue remediation, suspend onboarding, and recommend rejection or termination where exposure exceeds appetite.

## 4. Objectives
- Maintain a complete and accurate third-party inventory.
- Apply proportional due diligence before commitment or data/system access.
- Ensure contracts contain risk-based protections and enforceable obligations.
- Continuously monitor critical and high-risk relationships.
- Coordinate third-party incidents, concentration risk, resilience, and exit plans.
- Produce reliable management and board reporting.

## 5. Principles
Risk-based; lifecycle-integrated; evidence-driven; independent challenge; least privilege; defense in depth; shared accountability; traceability; proportionality; continuous improvement.

## 6. Governance forums
- **Board/Risk Committee:** appetite, material exposures, systemic concentration.
- **Executive TPRM Steering Committee:** monthly oversight and exceptions.
- **Third-Party Risk Working Group:** operational decisions and escalations.
- **Business Owner Reviews:** performance, risk, renewal, and exit readiness.

## 7. Success criteria
Coverage, assessment timeliness, critical-vendor monitoring, overdue issue reduction, contract clause compliance, incident notification performance, exit-plan testing, concentration visibility, and audit finding closure.
