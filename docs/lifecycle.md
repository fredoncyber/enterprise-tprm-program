# Third-Party Lifecycle

## 1. Plan and inventory
Define service need, accountable owner, alternatives, data flows, assets, jurisdictions, fourth parties, critical processes, recovery requirements, and exit strategy.

## 2. Intake and tiering
Complete intake before RFP selection or contract execution. Determine inherent risk and required review domains.

## 3. Due diligence
Issue targeted questionnaires and evidence requests. Validate scope, freshness, exceptions, complementary user-entity controls, subservice organizations, and inconsistencies.

## 4. Risk analysis
Document risk statements, affected objectives, control gaps, likelihood, impact, velocity, concentration, evidence confidence, and residual risk.

## 5. Contracting
Translate findings into clauses, service levels, security schedules, data terms, audit rights, incident obligations, resilience requirements, remediation dates, and termination assistance.

## 6. Onboarding
Verify approved architecture, identities, least privilege, logging, data minimization, integration security, asset ownership, operational contacts, and monitoring enrollment before production.

## 7. Monitoring
Monitor cyber events, vulnerabilities, external posture, financial/legal changes, control attestations, SLA performance, fourth-party changes, geographic exposure, incidents, and remediation.

## 8. Change and renewal
Reassess upon material change: acquisition, new subprocessor, platform redesign, new data class, new geography, major incident, control failure, AI functionality, or service expansion.

## 9. Termination
Revoke access, recover assets, export records, validate deletion, preserve legal records, remove integrations, reconcile accounts, transfer service, and capture lessons learned.
